### A2. ⭐⭐⭐ Product Owner (PO) — "the voice of the customer"
📘 Who-am-I: "I'm responsible for deciding what items will go into the product, and I have the authority to accept them as 'Done' on behalf of the company."
**Responsibilities**
1. **Owns and manages the Product Backlog** — "making sure it is accurate, up-to-date, and prioritized by business value" (📘 who-is-responsible key).
2. **Maximises value** — "I'm what the Product Owner helps the team optimize and maximize" → **Value** (📘).
3. Defines the **product vision and product goal**; communicates it.
4. Writes/clarifies **user stories** and **acceptance criteria**; answers team questions daily.
5. **Refines** the backlog continuously (📘 "What POs routinely do to keep the product backlog current" → **Refine**).
6. **Provides due dates** for the project and/or its releases (📘); release planning.
7. **Accepts or rejects** completed increments at the sprint review — "determines whether each completed product increment is working as intended, and either accepts it or requests changes" (📘).
8. Represents stakeholders; **one person, not a committee**; has authority to make product decisions.
9. Can **cancel a sprint** if the sprint goal becomes obsolete (📘: allowed but wasteful).
**Characteristics:** available, decisive, domain knowledge, empowered, good communicator.
**Example:** In a bank's mobile-app project, the digital banking head is PO: decides UPI autopay ranks above dark mode because more customers asked for it.

### A3. ⭐⭐⭐ Scrum Master (SM) — "servant leader"
📘 Who-am-I: "I'm a servant leader who guides the team in understanding and implementing Scrum, and helps people outside of the team grasp it." Answer key: "The Scrum Master guides the team past roadblocks and helps them implement Scrum."
**Responsibilities**
1. **Servant leader** to the delivery team — "helping them improve and removing barriers to their process" (📘).
2. **Removes impediments** raised in daily scrum.
3. **Facilitates meetings** — planning, reviews and retrospectives (📘).
4. **Coaches self-organisation** — "helps the delivery team self-govern and self-organize, instead of governing and organizing them" (📘).
5. **Shields the team** from interruptions and outside pressure.
6. **Teaches Scrum** to the team, PO and organisation; ensures Scrum rules and timeboxes are followed.
7. Helps the PO with backlog techniques; promotes transparency (charts, boards).
8. Tracks the burndown (📘 MTF: velocity numbers are not only the SM's job; the whole team should care — the statement "that's the Scrum Master's job" is **Not constructive**).
**The SM is NOT** a project manager who assigns tasks, nor the team's boss.

**📘 Servant leadership (p8)** — four duties:
- Shield the team from interruptions
- Remove impediments to progress
- Communicate (and re-communicate) the project vision
- **Carry food and water** (take care of the team's needs: tools, environment, morale)

📘 Classify (servant leadership vs command-and-control): "Suggestions" box on the wall → **Servant**; doing admin work for team members → **Servant**; posting the Gantt chart, handing out detailed task lists, creating the WBS alone over a weekend → **Command-and-control**.

### A4. ⭐⭐⭐ Development Team (Developers / Delivery Team)
📘 Who-am-I: "I'm the group of professionals who actually do all of the work that's needed to deliver the software to the users and stakeholders."
**Responsibilities**
1. **Build the increment** that meets the Definition of Done every sprint.
2. **Self-organising:** decide how to turn backlog items into an increment; decompose into tasks; volunteer (pull) work.
3. **Cross-functional:** all skills (design, code, test, analysis) in the team; no sub-teams or titles (📗 Scrum Guide).
4. **Estimate** their own work (📘 T/F: "Agile teams create their own estimates" — TRUE; "Agile estimates are created by the PO" — FALSE).
5. **Write acceptance tests** for the increments (📘).
6. **Demonstrate** the completed increment to the customer in the review meeting (📘).
7. Hold the **daily scrum**; update the task board and burndown.
8. Size: 📗 small (3–9 developers; whole Scrum team ≤ 10).

### A5. Project Sponsor (📘)
Main advocate and funder; "focuses on the big picture of whether the project will deliver the expected value on time and on budget" (📘). Removes organisational obstacles; approves charter.

### A6. ⭐⭐⭐ How the roles collaborate (Presentation Topic 6)
| Event | Product Owner | Scrum Master | Development Team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Backlog refinement | Leads; explains, orders | Facilitates | Estimates, asks questions |
| Sprint planning | Proposes goal and top items (what/why) | Facilitates, timeboxes | Selects items, decomposes into tasks (how) |
| Daily scrum | Optional (listens) | Ensures it happens, removes impediments | Runs it; 3 questions |
| Sprint review | Accepts/rejects; updates backlog | Facilitates | Demos the increment |
| Retrospective | Participates | Facilitates | Participates, owns actions |

### A7. Comparison table (frequently asked) ⭐⭐⭐
| Basis | Product Owner | Scrum Master | Development Team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Focus | What to build, why (value) | How Scrum works (process) | How to build (product) |
| Owns | Product backlog | Scrum process, (📘 sprint backlog) | Increment, tasks |
| Accountable for | ROI, priorities, acceptance | Team effectiveness, impediments | Quality, Done increment |
| Leadership type | Business leader | Servant leader / coach | Self-organising peers |
| Number | One | One (may serve several teams) | 3–9 |

| Analogy (film) | Producer | Director's assistant / coach | Crew and actors |

### A8. 📘 Leadership vs Management (p9)
| Management focus | Leadership focus |
|---|---|
| Tasks / things | People |
| Control | Empowerment |
| Efficiency | Effectiveness |
| Doing things right | Doing the right things |
| Speed | Direction |
| Practices | Principles |
| Command | Communication |
📘 Classify: planning workshops, team brainstorming, career planning, team-member recognition → **Leadership**; creating Gantt charts, HR management, team time tracking, task assignment → **Management**.

### A9. Coaching and conflict (📘 p25, p36)
- **Coaching levels:** individual coaching (mid-iteration, helps individuals develop agile and problem-solving skills) and team coaching (during iteration planning at the start and during the retrospective at the end).
- **Conflict levels (Speed Leas):**

| Level | Name | Characteristic | Resolution | 📘 example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem to solve | Open, fact-based; information sharing | Collaboration & consensus | "How do I request a ticket again?" |
| 2 | Disagreement | Guarded, self-protection | Empower team members | "I'll have extra work too if we use your method" |
| 3 | Contest | Trying to win; personal attacks; blame game | Accommodate, compromise | "You never care about anyone else!" |
| 4 | Crusade | Protect own group, remove the other side; ideological | De-escalate downwards | "We'd be better off without them!" |
| 5 | World war | "Destroy the other side!"; little or no communication | Separate parties & live with it | "You and me, outside, right now!" |

## Part B — ⭐⭐⭐ Team Space and High-Touch, Low-Tech Tools

### B1. Team space (physical space recommendations) 📘 + 📗
📘 XP concepts (p38): **informative workspace, osmotic communication, energized work, information radiators, sustainable pace**; XP practice **"Sit together"** (📘 Who-am-I: "a practice which makes sure the team has a space where everyone is near their teammates").
**Recommendations**
1. **Co-locate** the whole team (including PO when possible) in one open area.
2. **Caves and commons** (📗, Cockburn/XP):
   - **Commons** = shared open area where the team works together, overhears conversations (osmotic communication), pairs, holds stand-ups.
   - **Caves** = small private rooms/quiet zones for focused work, private calls, or thinking.
3. **Walls covered with information radiators:** task board, burndown, release plan, DoD, working agreements.
4. **Whiteboards and sticky notes** everywhere; space for daily stand-up in front of the board.
5. **Pair-programming stations** (two chairs, one screen).
6. Minimise interruptions; "energized work" environment (natural light, snacks, quiet hours).
7. **Distributed teams:** virtual team space — video always on, digital boards (Jira, Trello, Miro), shared chat.
```
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  [Cave 1]  [Cave 2]          WALL: Task board | Burndown | DoD │
 │                                                               │
 │        COMMONS: team desks in a cluster, pairing stations     │
 │        ┌────┬────┬────┐   ┌────┬────┬────┐                    │
 │        │ D1 │ D2 │ D3 │   │ D4 │ D5 │ PO │   ← stand-up spot  │
 │        └────┴────┴────┘   └────┴────┴────┘     facing board   │
 │  [Cave 3 / meeting]     Whiteboard          Coffee & snacks   │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
- **Osmotic communication:** "a great way to absorb information about the project from discussions happening around me" (📘). Team members pick up useful information by overhearing, without formal meetings.
- **Informative workspace:** "a team space that does a great job communicating information about the project" (📘).

### B2. ⭐⭐⭐ Information radiators
📘 Who-am-I: "I'm a large burndown chart or task board put up in the team space in a spot where everyone can't help but notice me." Answer key: "A burndown chart or task board posted where you can't help but absorb its data is an information **radiator**."
- Term by **Alistair Cockburn**: a display that **radiates** information to anyone walking by (vs an "information refrigerator" hidden in a tool/folder).
- **Examples:** task board, Kanban board, burndown/burnup chart, CFD, release plan, impediment list, build status light (red/green lamp), team working agreement, DoD poster, niko-niko (mood) calendar.
- **Characteristics:** big, visible, simple, current (updated daily), low-tech, readable at a glance, public.
- **Benefits:** transparency (Scrum pillar), shared understanding, early problem detection, fewer status meetings and reports, accountability, stakeholder trust.

### B3. ⭐⭐⭐ Task board (📘 p18 GASP, p20)
📘 MTF key: "A task board is an agile planning tool in which user stories are attached to a board and categorized into columns based on their status." Another key: "A tool for visualizing the progress of individual work items through an iteration."
```
 ┌───────────────┬───────────────┬───────────────┬───────────────┐
 │  STORY        │    TO DO      │  IN PROGRESS  │     DONE      │
 ├───────────────┼───────────────┼───────────────┼───────────────┤
 │ Story 1:      │ [Design UI]   │ [Write API]   │ [DB schema]   │
 │ Login (5 pts) │ [Unit tests]  │   (Asha)      │               │
 ├───────────────┼───────────────┼───────────────┼───────────────┤
 │ Story 2:      │ [Cart screen] │               │ [Wireframe]   │
 │ Cart (8 pts)  │ [Tests]       │               │               │
 └───────────────┴───────────────┴───────────────┴───────────────┘
   Reset at the start of every sprint; shows the sprint backlog only.
```
- One row per story (swimlane), tasks as sticky notes; moved during the daily scrum (📘 do's: "Create a new sticky note for each new task that is started").
- Belongs to **Scrum**: time-boxed to one sprint; board is cleared each sprint.

### B4. ⭐⭐⭐ Kanban board (📘 p46–48)
- **Kanban** = "signal card" in Japanese (📘). A method for improving process that implements a **pull system**.
- **Core practices (📘 p47):** Visualize workflow; Limit WIP; Manage flow; Make process policies explicit; Implement feedback loops; Improve collaboratively, evolve experimentally.
```
 ┌──────────┬──────────┬────────────┬───────────┬────────┬───────────┐
 │ BACKLOG  │ DEFINE   │ BUILD/TEST │ INTEGRATE │ UAT    │ COMPLETE  │
 │          │  (WIP 2) │  (WIP 3)   │  (WIP 2)  │ (WIP 2)│           │
 ├──────────┼──────────┼────────────┼───────────┼────────┼───────────┤
 │ [F7]     │ [F5]     │ [F3] [F4]  │ [F2]      │ [F1]   │ [F0]      │
 │ [F8]     │ [F6]     │ [  ]       │ [  ]      │        │           │
 │ [F9]     │          │            │           │        │           │
 └──────────┴──────────┴────────────┴───────────┴────────┴───────────┘
   ◄────────── work is PULLED from the left only when there is space (WIP limit)
```
- **WIP limit:** maximum cards allowed in a column. If "Build/Test" has 3 cards and limit is 3, nobody starts new work; they help finish (stop starting, start finishing).
- **Continuous flow:** no sprints; board is never reset.
- 📘 "**Busy highway vs free highway**: Optimize **throughput**, not **resource utilization**." A highway at 100% utilisation is a traffic jam; WIP limits keep work flowing.

### B5. ⭐⭐⭐ Task board vs Kanban board (Presentation Topic 12; 📘 p48)
| Basis | Task board (Scrum) | Kanban board |
|---|---|---|
| Columns | Simple: To Do, In Progress, Done | Reflect actual process steps (Define, Build, Test, Integrate, UAT, Complete) |
| WIP limits | None (implicitly limited by sprint commitment) | Explicit WIP limit on each column |
| Cadence | Time-boxed: sprint; board reset every sprint | Continuous flow; board persists |
| Work items | Tasks from sprint backlog (stories decomposed) | Features / work items / tickets |
| Changes mid-cycle | Avoided during sprint (protect sprint goal) | Allowed any time if capacity exists |
| Roles | PO, SM, Dev team | No prescribed roles |
| Key metric | Velocity, burndown | Lead time, cycle time, throughput, CFD |
| System | Commitment for the sprint (team selects batch) | Pull system per item |
| Best for | Product development in iterations | Support, maintenance, operations, service desks, flow work |
| Real-life examples | Software sprint, college event sprint | IT helpdesk, hospital patient flow, content publishing, Toyota assembly line, CMMS work orders |

**Benefits common to both:** visualise work, transparency, self-organisation, spot bottlenecks, information radiator.
**Scrumban:** a hybrid using Scrum events with Kanban WIP limits.

### B6. Other high-touch, low-tech tools (Presentation Topic 11)
Sticky notes and index cards (story cards), physical burndown on flip chart, planning-poker cards, dot voting, fist of five, niko-niko calendar, parking lot board, impediment board, wireframes on paper (📘 p38: "keep them low fidelity"), persona posters.
**Why low-tech:** cheap, fast, tactile (encourages participation), always visible, easy to change, no training needed.
