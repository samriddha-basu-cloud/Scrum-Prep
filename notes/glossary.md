# Glossary — Agile & Scrum Definitions A–Z
Exam-ready one- or two-line definitions for every term in the course, plus common extras. 📘 = wording from the faculty slides or answer keys; 📗 = textbook/standard definition. Use the flashcards view to self-test.

## A
- **Acceptance criteria** 📗: Conditions a user story must meet to be accepted by the Product Owner, often written as Given–When–Then.
- **Acceptance test-driven development (ATDD)** 📘: Writing acceptance tests with the customer before coding, so the tests define "done"; a verification and validation practice.
- **Adjourning** 📘: Fifth Tuckman stage, when the team disbands at the end of the project.
- **Affinity estimating** 📗: Team silently groups many stories by relative size on a wall to estimate quickly.
- **Agile** 📘: A set of methods and methodologies made of practices, and also a mindset guided by values and principles.
- **Agile Manifesto** 📘: The 2001 statement of 4 values and 12 principles for better ways of developing software, written by 17 practitioners at Snowbird, Utah.
- **Agile coach** 📗: Person who helps teams and organisations adopt agile ways of working; the Scrum Master often plays this role.
- **Andon** 📗: Toyota signal (cord or light) any worker can use to stop the line when a problem appears; the agile parallel is stopping to fix a broken build.
- **Architectural spike** 📘: A small throwaway program written as an experiment to test a technical approach.
- **Artifacts (Scrum)** 📘: Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog and Increment.

## B
- **Backlog** 📘: The list of features that haven't been built yet, prioritised with the most valuable at the top.
- **Backlog grooming / refinement** 📘: Adding, removing, re-estimating and re-prioritising items in the backlog to keep it current.
- **Burndown chart** 📘: A line chart, updated daily, that tracks the amount of work left, burning down to zero when the work is done.
- **Burnup chart** 📘: A chart that tracks total scope and completed work on separate lines, keeping progress and scope separate.
- **Business value** 📗: The benefit (revenue, cost saving, risk reduction, customer satisfaction) an item delivers; the PO orders the backlog by it.

## C
- **Caves and commons** 📗: Team-space design with shared open areas for collaboration (commons) and small private spaces for focused work (caves).
- **Ceremonies / events** 📗: The Scrum meetings: Sprint, Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective.
- **Collective commitment** 📘: Everyone on the Scrum team feels responsible for the whole project ("pigs", not "chickens").
- **Common-cause variation** 📘: Normal day-to-day differences due to normal factors; fix the system, not the event.
- **Continuous integration (CI)** 📘: Integrating and testing code many times a day so problems surface immediately.
- **Cost of delay** 📘: The economic loss from delivering something later; a Lean thinking tool used for prioritisation.
- **Courage** 📘: Scrum/XP value of doing the right thing and telling the truth, e.g. refusing a deadline you know you can't meet.
- **Cross-functional team** 📗: A team that has all the skills needed to deliver a Done increment without depending on outsiders.
- **Cumulative Flow Diagram (CFD)** 📗: A stacked area chart of the number of items in each workflow state over time; band thickness shows WIP, horizontal width shows cycle time.
- **Customer collaboration** 📘: Third Agile value: working with the customer as a partner, valued over contract negotiation.
- **Cycle time** 📘: Time from when work on an item starts until it is done.

## D
- **Daily Scrum** 📘: A 15-minute timeboxed daily meeting where developers plan the next 24 hours using three questions.
- **Decompose** 📘: What the team does to turn Sprint Backlog items into tasks.
- **Definition of Done (DoD)** 📘: An agreed-upon condition which, when satisfied, means a feature is complete.
- **Definition of Ready (DoR)** 📗: Checklist a backlog item must meet before it enters a sprint (clear, estimated, small, has acceptance criteria).
- **DEEP** 📗: A good product backlog is Detailed appropriately, Estimated, Emergent and Prioritised.
- **Development Team** 📘: The group of professionals who actually do all the work needed to deliver the product.
- **DevOps** 📗: Culture and practices that unite development and operations for continuous delivery and deployment.
- **Dot voting** 📘: Prioritisation where each person places a fixed number of dots on the options they value most.

## E
- **Elapsed time** 📘: Calendar time to finish work, including interruptions.
- **Empiricism** 📘: The theory that knowledge comes from experience; Scrum's basis, built on transparency, inspection and adaptation.
- **Energized work** 📘: XP practice of working with rested minds at a sustainable pace.
- **Epic** 📗: A large user story too big for one sprint, split into smaller stories.
- **ESVP** 📘: Retrospective check-in poll: Explorers, Shoppers, Vacationers, Prisoners.
- **Expected Monetary Value (EMV)** 📘: Probability × impact of a risk, used to order risks in the risk-adjusted backlog.
- **Exploratory testing** 📘: Activities to verify whether or not a particular approach will work.

## F
- **Face-to-face conversation** 📘: The most efficient and effective method of conveying information (Principle 6).
- **Fast failure / fail fast** 📘: Learning about risks and wrong assumptions early, when failure is cheap.
- **Fibonacci scale** 📗: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21… used for story points because uncertainty grows with size.
- **Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram** 📘: Cause-and-effect diagram used to find the root cause of issues.
- **Fist of five** 📘: Quick consensus vote showing 0–5 fingers.
- **Flow efficiency** 📘: Faculty formula: cycle time ÷ lead time. (Many books: active work time ÷ lead time.)
- **Focus** 📘: Scrum value: you don't even consider doing two things at once.
- **Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing** 📘: Tuckman's team stages, matched with Directing, Coaching, Supporting, Delegating leadership.

## G
- **GASPs** 📘: Generally Accepted Scrum Practices: timebox, user stories, story points, task boards, planning poker, burndown chart, persona.
- **Gemba** 📗: "The real place": go and see where the work happens; agile parallel is the sprint review with real users.
- **Gold plating** 📗: Adding features nobody asked for; a form of the "extra features" waste.
- **Gulf of evaluation** 📘: The gap between what was described and what was interpreted.

## H
- **Hack / kludge** 📘: A clumsy, quick-and-dirty solution.
- **Heijunka** 📗: Toyota's levelling of production volume and mix; agile parallel is a steady sprint cadence.
- **High-touch, low-tech** 📗: Using simple physical tools (sticky notes, whiteboards) that encourage collaboration.
- **Hybrid** 📘: Mixing approaches, e.g. XP with a full Scrum implementation, or predictive hardware with agile software.

## I
- **Ideal time** 📘: Time required to accomplish a task if it is done without interruption.
- **Impediment** 📗: Anything that blocks the team's progress; the Scrum Master removes it.
- **Increment** 📘: The sum of all items delivered at the end of a sprint; deliverable only if every item is Done.
- **Incremental design** 📘: Making design decisions at the last responsible moment so code stays easy to change.
- **Information radiator** 📘: A large burndown chart or task board posted where everyone can't help but notice it.
- **Informative workspace** 📘: A team space that communicates information about the project.
- **INVEST** 📘: A good user story is Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimatable, Small, Testable.
- **Iteration** 📘: Repeatedly performing all project activities in small chunks; a sprint in Scrum.

## J
- **Jidoka** 📗: "Automation with a human touch": stop as soon as a defect appears; a pillar of TPS.
- **Just-in-time (JIT)** 📗: Make only what is needed, when it is needed, in the amount needed; a pillar of TPS.

## K
- **Kaizen** 📘: Japanese for continuous improvement; retrospectives are Agile's Kaizen.
- **Kanban** 📘: Japanese for "signal card"; a method that improves process using a pull system and WIP limits.
- **Kanban board** 📘: Board with columns for each workflow step and WIP limits per column.
- **Kano model** 📘: Classifies features as basic (must-be), performance and delighters.

## L
- **Last responsible moment** 📘: Make decisions when you have the most information, before delay causes harm.
- **Lead time** 📘: Time from when a request enters the system until it is delivered.
- **Lean** 📘: A mindset (not a methodology) based on principles: eliminate waste, amplify learning, decide late, deliver fast, empower the team, build quality in, see the whole.
- **Little's Law** 📗: Average cycle time = average WIP ÷ average throughput.

## M
- **Mindset** 📘: What makes the biggest difference when adopting agile practices.
- **Minimal Marketable Feature (MMF)** 📘: A small, self-contained feature that delivers value; focus on earning.
- **Minimum Viable Product (MVP)** 📘: A small feature set offered to customers to learn their actual behaviour; focus on learning.
- **MoSCoW** 📘: Prioritisation into Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have (this time).
- **Muda, Mura, Muri** 📗: Waste, unevenness and overburden in Lean.

## N
- **Niko-niko calendar** 📗: A wall calendar where team members mark their daily mood.

## O
- **Openness** 📘: Scrum value of being transparent about work and problems.
- **Options thinking** 📘: Keeping choices open until the last responsible moment.
- **Osmotic communication** 📘: Absorbing information about the project from discussions happening around you.

## P
- **Pair programming** 📘: Two people working at one computer: one drives, one navigates.
- **Persona** 📘: A made-up user with a name and personal facts that helps the team understand who its users are.
- **Pigs and chickens** 📘: Fable: pigs (the Scrum team) are committed; chickens (stakeholders) are only involved.
- **Planning poker** 📘: Estimation game where members reveal cards at once and discuss high and low estimates until consensus.
- **PDCA vs PDEL** 📘: Kaizen follows Plan-Do-Check-Act; Agile follows Plan-Develop-Evaluate-Learn.
- **Poka-yoke** 📗: Mistake-proofing; agile parallel is automated tests.
- **Pre-mortem** 📘: Imagine the project has failed and work out what caused it.
- **Product Backlog** 📘: An ordered list of all items (with descriptions, estimates and values) that might be needed in the product.
- **Product Owner** 📘: Decides what goes into the product, orders the backlog by value and accepts work as Done.
- **Product roadmap** 📗: High-level, time-based view of how the product will evolve toward its vision.
- **Progressive elaboration** 📘: Developing an artifact in steps, improving it with knowledge from the previous step.
- **Project** 📘: A temporary endeavour undertaken to create a unique product, service or result (PMBOK).
- **Project Sponsor** 📘: The project's main advocate, focused on whether it will deliver value on time and on budget.
- **Pull system** 📘: Each later step pulls work from the previous step when it has capacity.

## Q
- **Quarterly cycle** 📘: XP's long-term planning, meeting users once a quarter to work on the backlog.
- **Queuing theory** 📘: Analysing how work flows and waits in a system.

## R
- **RACI** 📘: Responsibility matrix: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.
- **Refactoring** 📘: Improving code structure without changing its behaviour.
- **Release planning** 📗: Deciding which backlog items go into a release and roughly when, using velocity.
- **Respect** 📘: Scrum/XP value of trusting teammates and valuing their contributions.
- **Retrospective** 📘: Meeting where the team inspects itself and plans improvements; stages: set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, close.
- **Rework** 📘: Changing previously written code to work differently; risky and best avoided.
- **Risk-adjusted backlog** 📘: A backlog that includes risk responses alongside features, ordered by value and EMV.
- **Risk-based spike** 📘: Experimental work to determine the impact of a specific potential problem or threat.
- **Risk burndown graph** 📘: Chart of the total daily estimated impact of problems, issues and threats.

## S
- **Scrum** 📘: The most common agile framework, focused on project management, with roles, events and artifacts.
- **Scrum Master** 📘: Servant leader who guides the team in understanding and implementing Scrum and removes impediments.
- **Scrum of Scrums** 📘: Coordination meeting of representatives from several Scrum teams on one product.
- **Scrum values** 📘: Openness, Respect, Courage, Focus, Commitment.
- **Scrumban** 📗: Hybrid of Scrum events with Kanban WIP limits and flow.
- **Self-organizing team** 📘: A team that decides by itself how it will meet its goals.
- **Servant leadership** 📘: Leading by shielding the team, removing impediments, communicating the vision and "carrying food and water".
- **Shu-Ha-Ri** 📘: Learning stages: follow one master, learn from many masters, create your own approach.
- **Sit together** 📘: XP practice ensuring everyone sits near their teammates.
- **Slack** 📘: Optional or minor items added to give the team breathing room.
- **Special-cause variation** 📘: Larger differences due to special or new factors; fix the specific event.
- **Spike** 📗: A timeboxed research or experiment task to reduce uncertainty.
- **Sprint** 📘: Fixed timebox of 2 weeks to 30 days in which a Done increment is created.
- **Sprint Backlog** 📘: The items the team will build in the sprint plus the plan (tasks) to build them.
- **Sprint Goal** 📘: Objective crafted by the team during sprint planning.
- **Sprint Planning** 📘: Timeboxed meeting where the team sets the sprint goal, selects items (part 1) and decomposes them into tasks (part 2).
- **Sprint Review** 📘: End-of-sprint inspection of the increment with invited users and stakeholders.
- **Stacey matrix** 📘: Requirements agreement vs technology certainty; agile works best in the complex zone.
- **Story map** 📗: 2-D arrangement of stories under a backbone of user activities, sliced into releases.
- **Story points** 📘: Relative, unitless measure of the size and complexity of a story.
- **Sustainable pace** 📘: A pace the team can maintain indefinitely (Principle 8).

## T
- **T-shaped person** 📘: Deep in one skill and broad in others; solves hand-off and bottleneck issues.
- **T-shirt sizing** 📘: Grouping features into small, medium and large effort categories.
- **Takt time** 📗: Available production time ÷ customer demand.
- **Task board** 📘: Board where stories and tasks move through To Do, In Progress and Done during an iteration.
- **Technical debt** 📘: Work needed to make code more maintainable in the long term.
- **Test-driven development (TDD)** 📘: Writing unit tests first, then code to pass them, then refactoring.
- **Throughput** 📗: Number of items finished per unit of time.
- **Timebox** 📘: A hard deadline with scope adjusted to meet it.
- **Toyota Production System (TPS)** 📗: Toyota's system built on JIT and Jidoka; the origin of Lean.
- **Transparency** 📘: Visibility of work and progress to all; the three daily scrum questions give it.

## U
- **User story** 📘: A short description of one specific user need: "As a <role>, I want <action>, so that <benefit>."
- **3Cs of a user story** 📘: Card, Conversation, Confirmation.

## V
- **Value stream mapping** 📘: Mapping every step from request to delivery to see how much time is spent waiting.
- **Velocity** 📘: The number of story points completed in a sprint.
- **Vision statement** 📗: Short statement of what the product is, for whom and why it matters.

## W
- **Waterfall** 📘: Sequential model where the project moves through phases and changes force it back to a prior phase.
- **Weekly cycle** 📘: XP's one-week iteration that delivers the next increment of Done software.
- **WIP limit** 📘: Maximum number of items allowed in a workflow step at once.
- **Wireframe** 📘: A simple sketch of a screen; keep it low fidelity.
- **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)** 📗: Hierarchical, deliverable-oriented decomposition of the total project scope.
- **WSJF** 📗: Weighted Shortest Job First = cost of delay ÷ job size (SAFe).

## X–Z
- **XP (Extreme Programming)** 📘: Agile methodology focused on software design and programming, with weekly iterations.
- **XP values** 📘: Courage, Respect, Communication, Feedback, Simplicity.
- **YAGNI** 📗: "You aren't gonna need it": don't build features until they are needed.
- **5S** 📘: Sort, Straighten, Shine, Standardize, Sustain.
- **5 Whys** 📗: Asking "why?" repeatedly (about five times) to reach a root cause.
